# Paper 5: Rebellion as a Coordination Game and Threshold Model

**Core game**: individual benefit vs. risk of joining a rebellion, a global game
**Tools**: threshold models, information cascades, coordination games, critical mass
**Show anchor**: riots, uprisings, bystanders, the cleaner event, Juliette's return
**Core thesis**: rebellion is not linear accumulation but threshold triggering; one public signal can ignite it
**Falsifiable corollary**: when public resisters exceed a critical count, suppression costs rise exponentially

---

## Abstract

What the administration of a closed system fears most is never one individual's discontent, but **when discontent turns into collective action.** This paper models that process with a coordination game and a threshold model, arguing a counterintuitive conclusion: a rebellion's outbreak is not a linear process of "grievance gradually accumulating," but a **threshold-triggered** process — it needs not just enough discontented people, but a **public enough signal** that makes every potential participant believe "others will join too." Once that signal appears, from "no one dares move" to "everyone moves," there is only a thin layer of tissue paper in between.

## 1. The Scene: From Bystanding to Uprising

Inside the silo, discontent is everywhere, but the moment that truly tips toward "uprising" is always triggered by a **public event** — a cleaner's public anomaly, a Juliette-style public challenge, a crowd surrounding one specific figure.

Before that moment, everyone believes they are an isolated minority; after that moment, everyone suddenly discovers: the people next door, upstairs, and downstairs were thinking the same thing.

## 2. The Model: The Threshold Model

Let each resident $i$ have a "participation threshold" $\theta_i$: they will join only if they believe the fraction of the population joining the rebellion exceeds $\theta_i$. The distribution of residents' thresholds characterizes the system's "flammability."

**The coordination game's key**: the benefit of participating rises with participant count (strength in numbers, and safety in numbers), while the risk falls with participant count (the crowd cannot all be punished). So there exists a **critical mass** $m^*$: once participation exceeds $m^*$, joining a rebellion shifts from "dangerous" to "rational."

**Information cascade**: residents cannot directly observe the true participant count; they can only infer it through **public signals** (who stood up, who was arrested, who was not). A sufficiently strong public signal makes a large number of residents with different thresholds cross them **simultaneously** — this is the "avalanche."

## 3. Equilibrium Analysis: Why Suppression Accelerates Collapse

The administration's first reaction to rebellion is usually **suppression** — public arrests, public executions, killing one to warn many.

But suppression is double-edged:

- It **reduces** the count of publicly active participants (physically removing them);
- But it **simultaneously** sends a stronger signal: *"so many people wanted to resist that the administration had to use violence."*

That signal makes the still-watching residents realize "I am not a minority," thereby lowering their participation thresholds. The result may be: **the harder the suppression, the more people join.**

This is the essence of the threshold model: the administration thinks it is putting out a fire, but it is pouring fuel on it.

## 4. Show Verification

In the show, every "cleansing" produces new rebels. The administration executes one cleaner and gains ten secret sympathizers; destroys one piece of history and gains ten word-of-mouth memories. Juliette's "return" — a living person who has seen the truth, appearing in public — is the strongest public signal of all: it pulls countless people's participation thresholds below the critical point in one stroke.

## 5. Falsifiable Corollary

> **In a closed system with widespread latent discontent, once a "public, not-instantly-suppressed resistance signal" appears, the fraction of the population joining the rebellion grows non-linearly, and after crossing critical mass enters an irreversible avalanche; every subsequent act of suppression further lowers potential participants' thresholds, accelerating rather than preventing collapse.**

The converse is falsifiable: if the administration can eliminate the signal **completely and invisibly** at the first instant (so that other residents never even learn a resistance occurred), the avalanche can be delayed. But "eliminating invisibly" is itself a race against the propagation speed of a "public signal" — and propagation is almost always faster.

## Conclusion

The mathematics of rebellion is not a question of "how many are discontented," but "how many know that others are discontented too." **Coordination is the real threshold of collective action.** And once that threshold is crossed by a public signal, everything after it is beyond the administration's control.
