# Paper 7: Fertility Licensing and Biopolitical Control

**Core game**: administration controls fertility quotas vs. residents' reproductive will
**Tools**: resource-constrained games, quota mechanisms, principal-agent, generational games
**Show anchor**: fertility lottery, population control, family separation
**Core thesis**: fertility control is both resource management and a power instrument; it manufactures long-run resistance motives
**Falsifiable corollary**: the stronger the fertility inequity, the higher the generational rebellion probability

---

## Abstract

A closed system's population must be held within resource limits — that is the surface logic of fertility licensing. But *Silo* reveals a deeper fact: **fertility control is never just resource management; it is an instrument of power.** Who is allowed to reproduce and who is stripped of that right is itself the source of the deepest, generationally transmitted resentment. This paper argues: fertility control looks like "maintaining system stability," but in reality it **manufactures the most indigestible resistance motive a system can create** — because nothing makes a person abandon identification with a system more completely than "you do not even have the right to leave descendants."

## 1. The Scene: A Lottery Decides Who May Live On

The silo enforces strict fertility licensing: a couple must win a "fertility quota" through a lottery to reproduce. The winners may have descendants; the losers are stripped of not just a child, but of "continuing oneself" — the most fundamental human right.

On the surface, this is rational allocation under resource constraints. But the lottery's randomness conceals one fact: **the rules of who wins and who loses are themselves designed by the administration.**

## 2. The Model: Fertility as a Bargaining Chip

Let the administration's goal be "maintaining stability under resource constraints." Fertility control, as an instrument, has this game structure:

- **Surface goal**: control population to match resources;
- **Real goal**: turn "the right to reproduce" into a **distributable chip**, used to reward obedience and punish dissent.

**Principal-agent view**: the administration (principal) uses fertility quotas (agent compensation) to purchase residents' obedience. Residents obey → get fertility rights; residents dissent → lose them.

But this mechanism has a fatal flaw: **the deprivation of fertility rights is an irreversible, generationally accumulating resentment.** A person stripped of the right to reproduce does not take their resentment to the grave — it passes to everyone around them who witnessed their fate.

## 3. Equilibrium Analysis: Short-Run Stability, Long-Run Bomb

Fertility control is "effective" in the short run: it does control population, and it does make people obey for "wanting descendants."

But it plants the deepest bomb in the long run:

- **The deprived**: lose all identification with the system, becoming potential, never-turning-back resisters;
- **The winners**: fear their luck will be revoked, living in constant anxiety, their loyalty to the system twisted;
- **The observers**: watching the lottery's randomness and inequity, realize "this system's rules can arbitrarily decide a person's continuation," and their trust in the system is fundamentally shaken.

**Fertility control produces not obedience, but a false stability of "everyone is afraid, but no one truly identifies."** The price of this stability is a monotonically rising generational rebellion probability.

## 4. Show Verification

In the show, the fertility lottery is one of the residents' deepest wounds. It splits "living" and "continuing" into two separate things — you may live, but you may not leave descendants. This splitting is precisely the administration's deepest violation of human nature, and therefore the deepest tinder of rebellion.

## 5. Falsifiable Corollary

> **In a closed system practicing fertility-quota control, the greater the inequity of quota allocation, the higher the system's generational rebellion probability, and this resistance motive accumulates monotonically over time, ultimately becoming one of the deepest drivers of breakout.**

The converse is falsifiable: if quota allocation were perfectly fair, transparent, and universally accepted as "inevitable under resource constraints," fertility control would not necessarily trigger rebellion. But "perfect fairness" is nearly impossible in an information-asymmetric closed system — because the judgment of fairness rests precisely in the administration's hands.

## Conclusion

Fertility control is the most dangerous move in a closed system: it uses "the right to continue" as a bargaining chip, buying short-term obedience while planting the deepest, generationally transmitted, never-dissolving resistance. **The moment a system starts controlling "who is allowed to continue," it has already written the reason for opposing itself into every person's genes.**
